Fixing a $9,063.35 opening balance gap in Xero

A property business had a bank account that showed as fully reconciled in Xero, yet the balance was thousands of dollars off from the real bank statement.

Service
Bookkeeping clean-up
Software
Xero
Client
Property business LLC
Focus
Bank reconciliation, opening balances

The problem

While cleaning up the client's business checking account, I compared the December 2024 bank statement with Xero. The statement closed at $14,582.90. Xero showed a reconciled balance of $5,519.55.

That left a historical gap of $9,063.35, and the same gap carried through every 2025 transaction. Every imported transaction was already marked reconciled, and the earlier bank statements that would reveal the source of the difference were not available.

What I did

  1. Compared statement to ledgerMatched the December 2024 closing balance against the Xero account transactions and confirmed the exact size of the gap.
  2. Checked the Xero historyConfirmed the existing transactions were reconciled and that the variance was constant, pointing to a missing opening balance rather than a bad entry.
  3. Asked the client before postingExplained the finding in plain language and asked them to confirm the adjustment and which account it should be allocated to.
  4. Worked around a Xero limitXero does not let a manual journal post directly to a bank account, so I added a manual transaction for the adjustment and marked it as reconciled.

The correction

The adjustment is a prior-period entry that raises the Xero bank balance by exactly the missing amount.

AccountDebitCredit
Bank account (business checking)$9,063.35
Opening Balance Equity / Prior Period Adjustment$9,063.35

Why a manual transaction? Xero blocks journals against bank accounts, so the same entry is recorded as a bank transaction against the equity account, then reconciled so the account stays clean.

How I verified it

Xero balance after imported transactions-$437.63
Opening balance adjustment+$9,063.35
Corrected Xero balance$8,625.72

The corrected balance matches the bank balance calculated independently from the client's January to October transactions, so the account now ties out.

Duplicate bank lines and unapplied customer payments

A design studio whose cash balance and receivables looked far bigger than reality.

Service
Bookkeeping clean-up
Software
Xero
Focus
Bank feed duplicates, accounts receivable

The scenario

A freelance design studio imported CSV statements while the bank feed was also switched on, so the same lines landed twice. Customers had also paid invoices that were never matched, so Xero still showed them as unpaid.

  • The Xero bank balance was $6,214.50 higher than the statement
  • Accounts receivable looked far larger than it really was

How I would approach it

  1. Compare to the statementTie the Xero balance to the statement and measure the gap.
  2. Find the duplicatesSort by date and amount, then remove the extra imported lines rather than reconciling them.
  3. Apply payments to invoicesMatch each customer receipt to its invoice so paid invoices leave the receivables list.
  4. Reconcile and reviewReconcile to the statement and check the aged receivables report with the owner.

Why it matters: duplicates inflated the cash balance, and unapplied payments made the studio look like it was owed money that had already arrived.

Loan repayments coded entirely to expense

A landscaping company whose profit looked lower than it was, and whose loan never went down.

Service
Bookkeeping clean-up
Software
Xero
Focus
Loan accounting, profit accuracy

The scenario

A small landscaping company paid $1,250.00 a month on an equipment loan, and every payment was coded to Loan Expense. Only the interest portion is an expense. The rest reduces the loan.

  • Ten payments totaling $12,500.00 sat in expenses
  • The loan balance on the balance sheet had never gone down

How I would approach it

  1. Get the loan scheduleRequest the lender statement showing interest and principal for each payment.
  2. Split the totalInterest was $2,180.00 and principal was $10,320.00 across the ten payments.
  3. Post a reclassifying journalDebit the loan liability and credit Loan Expense for the principal. This is a normal journal, because it does not touch the bank account.
  4. Set up future paymentsCreate a repeating split so each new payment posts correctly.

Illustrative before and after

Year to dateBeforeAfter
Loan expense$12,500.00$2,180.00
Net profit$18,450.00$28,770.00
Loan balance$60,000.00$49,680.00

The adjustment: $10,320.00 moves out of expenses and reduces the loan, so profit rises by the same amount and the balance sheet reflects what is still owed.

Online shop with an unreviewed bank feed and a payment processor double count

A candle shop that had never reconciled, with sales counted twice.

Service
Bookkeeping clean-up
Software
QuickBooks Online
Focus
Bank feed, payment processor, categories

The scenario

The owner had never reconciled. The accountant needed clean books for tax filing and flagged three problems:

  • 140 bank feed transactions waiting in For Review
  • Stripe payouts counted twice, once as sales and again as bank deposits
  • $4,236.75 sitting in Uncategorized Expense

How I would approach it

  1. Request source documentsBank statements, the Stripe payout and fee report, and receipts for larger purchases.
  2. Fix the Stripe double countCreate a Stripe clearing account. Sales post to it, payouts move from it to the bank, and processing fees go to a merchant fees expense, so income is counted once.
  3. Work through For ReviewMatch bank feed lines to existing entries first, then categorize what remains and set bank rules for repeat vendors.
  4. Reclassify Uncategorized ExpenseSort each item into the right expense account, and flag personal spending for the owner to confirm as owner draws.
  5. Reconcile month by monthTie each month to its bank statement until the ending balance matches.

Service
Bookkeeping clean-up
Software

Focus

The scenario

A consulting firm had never reconciled its credit card, and transactions it was unsure about had been parked in the Ask My Accountant account. The chart of accounts had also grown messy.

  • $3,672.18 across 22 transactions parked in Ask My Accountant
  • QuickBooks showed $1,148.25 more owing on the card than the statement
  • 94 accounts, many of them near duplicates

How I would approach it

  1. Find the card differenceFour charges were entered twice, totaling $972.50, and one refund of $175.75 was never recorded.
  2. Empty the parking accountReview each parked item with receipts and move it to the right expense account.
  3. Tidy the chart of accountsMerge duplicate accounts and inactivate the ones no longer used.
  4. Reconcile the cardReconcile each month to the card statement until the balance matches.
